A-142: from scattered brake reports to a governed decision

When a reported brake fault repeats and becomes safety-critical, what evidence supports holding the asset from service, and who authorizes the decision?

Follow six synthetic reports through an evidence-backed review, a deterministic repeat-event rule and a recorded approval. Inspect the implementation behind each step before considering a workflow pilot.

What this demonstration establishes

  • Synthetic demonstration only: every organisation, person and operational report in this case is fictional.
  • Deterministic fixture intelligence returns authored, checksum-bound extraction objects. The 70% confidence is authored fixture data, not a measured live-model result.
  • The recording uses Playwright to exercise the application's authenticated human-governed review and approval controls. No maintenance lead was consulted.
  • Recorded approval authorizes a proposed decision. It does not establish physical removal, completed repairs, production deployment or measured savings.

consistent with the operator's description. Some grinding is audible

months, and the pad wear pattern is not consistent with normal use, this
looks more like a recurring issue than an isolated one - possibly a
sticking caliper or a slow line leak causing uneven pad wear and reduced
pedal firmness. Recommend a more thorough diagnostic beyond a simple pad
swap.

Follow-up diagnostic and repair should be scheduled within 5 business
days (by 2026-05-26) given the safety-relevant nature of a braking
system and the recurrence pattern. Unit may continue in service in the

feel.

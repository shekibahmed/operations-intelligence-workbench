# Document Assurance Scenario Pack — Narrative Fixtures

From fragmented operational information to accountable decisions: the
Document Assurance pack tells the story of a single engagement, Project
Falcon, in which a routine contract review surfaces two quiet but
material risk-exposure problems — a missing insurance certificate and a
liability cap that disagrees with internal policy by two and a half
times its stated figure. Left as scattered documents, emails, and
checklists, neither problem would be visible on its own; tracked as
obligations against a live case, they surface early, get escalated with
explicit conditions, and are closed out through a formally approved
exception rather than left to drift. For leadership, this is the
difference between a filing cabinet of contracts and a system that can
answer, at any point, what the organisation is actually exposed to and
who signed off on accepting that exposure.

A document review should produce tracked obligations, not only a
summary. Operationally, the Project Falcon storyline follows the
obligations set out in the Master Services Agreement — quarterly
compliance reporting, annual insurance certificate renewal, a liability
cap — from execution through two full quarterly review cycles. Review
Owner Nadia Okonkwo chases an ambiguous clarification from Vantage
Logistics Partners' legal contact, absorbs a legitimate mid-stream
amendment without losing track of the original obligation, identifies a
conflicting-provisions discrepancy by name, and formally proposes an
"accept exception" decision with explicit, scoped conditions that an
authorised reviewer, Marcus Ibori, reviews and approves on the record.
The smoke set shows what this same flow looks like when nothing goes
wrong — routine NDAs and service orders that close out cleanly with no
escalation — and the edge set stress-tests seven specific ways review
information can go missing, conflict, duplicate, mislead, or arrive
without enough evidence to act on.

Technically, each artifact in this pack is raw source material — a
contract, a policy, a checklist, an email, a reviewer note — with no
injected metadata, from which the pack expects a full evidence chain to
be derivable: observations extracted from the artifact text, an
operational event recording what happened, a signal where the content
warrants one (missing evidence, conflicting provisions, missed
deadlines, ambiguous entities, suspicious content, insufficient
evidence), a case linking related artifacts across the Project Falcon
timeline, and, where risk exposure crosses a threshold, a Decision with
an Approval record naming who approved it and under what conditions. The
filename is the stable artifact ID at every stage of that chain, and
`expected-outcomes.md` records, per artifact, exactly which of those
fields should be extractable, which should remain genuinely ambiguous,
and which are deliberately absent — the provenance a downstream system
needs to reproduce from raw text alone.

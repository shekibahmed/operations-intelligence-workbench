Grounding Context

My first pick: the public evidence casebook. It can give a reader something useful before local setup. My best use of spare tokens: the independently reviewed challenge corpus. It converts generation into durable evidence rather than a larger runtime system.

44 frame candidates + 3 combinations → 6 retained directions. All four topic axes have a survivor. Confidence percentages express editorial confidence in fit; they are not estimated conversion rates.

Codebase Context. This is a mature synthetic reference platform: three scenario packs, deterministic extraction, evidence and review, rules, cases, human approvals, audit, metrics, exports and extensive CI. Inspection is pinned to 6b45b5b. P0 scope is complete according to the repository records. No hosted demo is reported; hosting was deliberately deferred by the owner (SESSION.md:9).

The September 13 changes already add activation framing, progress milestones, a copyable summary, contextual conversion, scenario funnel aggregates, pack scaffolding, registration guidance, a container deployment runbook and an expiry workflow. SESSION.md:27 still describes some of that work as unfinished. Several public docs lag the code; these ideas account for shipped behavior rather than rediscovering it.

The real gaps are accessible public proof, an experiment that reveals why outcomes change, a comprehensible first contribution, and stronger independent evaluation references. The existing summary records scenario, entry and progress rather than the operational outcome. Pack visibility still has a separate web-owned handoff. The perfect deterministic score is not evidence of general live-model accuracy.

Product boundaries. Industry language and behavior stay in packs. Default examples remain synthetic and deterministic; providers do not write state or approve decisions. Shared contracts are frozen. Hosting, a public publishing destination or a contract extension would be a separate decision. These are directions to explore, not authorized implementation work.

Useful measures. Look for readers who inspect an evidence chain, finish a causal exercise, submit a distinct counterexample, or reach a first accepted contribution. Stars can indicate interest, but more commits or token consumption alone would not establish improvement. No traffic, conversion baseline or live issue inventory was measured in this pass.

1. A public evidence casebook

First encounter and public discoveryConfidence 90%Complexity Medium
Description
Give a browser-only reader one concrete synthetic decision to question before asking them to install anything. A compact casebook would expose the original passages, uncertain fact, evaluated rule, proposed decision, human approval and final outcome. A recorded exhibit could be readable inside GitHub or opened as a standalone replay; any separately published website is a later distribution choice. Each example would identify its pack version and generating revision, and say clearly that it demonstrates authored fixture behavior.
Basis
direct: The current quick start requires clone, install, Postgres, migrations and a running app (README.md:175). CI already exercises the lifecycle; evidence and rule inspectors exist. Workspace pages are intentionally excluded from crawling (apps/web/src/app/robots.ts:9). external: Langfuse’s example project connects an interaction to an inspectable trace. reasoned: A recorded, source-linked story can expose OIW’s reasoning without needing a live guest workspace.
Rationale
This is the strongest first direction for public interest. A reader gets useful evidence to inspect and a specific engineering lesson to share. Accepted fixture improvements can refresh the casebook, connecting public material to substantive repository work. The likely engagement benefit is a hypothesis, not a measured star-growth claim.
Downsides
A recorded replay can be mistaken for live AI unless its limits are prominent. Exported material needs a freshness link to the source revision. Start with a small set of compelling stories; a large generated article catalog would create maintenance work before demand is known.
Discussion test
Could someone who refuses to install Postgres dispute a decision, find the source passage and locate its verifying test?

2. Change one fact, watch the decision change

Visitor experiment and memorable operational storyConfidence 85%Complexity Medium
Description
Offer a finite set of paired synthetic examples. Ask visitors to predict what happens when a decisive sentence disappears, an entity reference becomes ambiguous, or a corroborating report conflicts. Reveal which observation becomes insufficient, which rule loses support and which proposal stays unavailable. An authored variant where refusing or requesting clarification is the correct human response would show the value of judgment. This extends the existing tour with a causal experiment rather than another faster tour.
Basis
direct: The rule inspector already exposes evaluated facts (apps/web/src/app/w/[workspace]/technical/rules/[id]/page.tsx:75), and artifact inspection displays insufficient evidence. The fixture provider only recognizes registered checksums (packages/intelligence/src/fixture-provider.ts:95). external: OPA’s debugging documentation describes policy/input experiments. reasoned: Paired authored fixtures preserve deterministic behavior while making a changed outcome explainable.
Rationale
A visitor can explain why the system must stop, rather than simply seeing badges and approving the expected proposal. The same paired examples could serve the demo, a technical article and a regression test. The novelty lies in revealing a boundary, not giving arbitrary text a fabricated fixture response.
Downsides
Both variants need carefully reviewed extraction and operational expectations. Stateful replay must distinguish current facts from historical decisions and keep guest isolation. Start as a small authored exercise; arbitrary editing, temporal simulation and a general rule builder would greatly enlarge the scope.
Discussion test
Can the visitor identify the one changed passage and explain the resulting review or decision boundary?

3. An independently reviewed challenge corpus

Technical credibility and learning from failuresConfidence 90%Complexity High
Description
Use spare tokens to draft candidate synthetic contradictions, unsupported claims, near-matching entity references, duplicates and instruction-like passages. Separate that drafting from judging: annotators inspect the source without seeing the fixture answer, then a human resolves disagreements about supported facts, evidence spans and exact expected operational outcomes. Keep only examples that introduce a distinct failure boundary. The lasting output is a curated, independently justified regression corpus that can also supply public challenge stories.
Basis
direct: packages/evals/README.md:47 documents that current gold lacks independent evidence locators and exact expected case state. Expected and actual evidence locators currently derive from fixture extraction (packages/evals/src/runner.ts:150). Existing sabotage and injection checks are real starting assets, not missing features. external: Promptfoo’s configuration separates adversarial generation from grading. reasoned: Independently justified references can reveal a shared error that fixture agreement alone cannot expose.
Rationale
This is the best use of abundant tokens. It attacks a documented evidence limitation and accumulates reusable knowledge without adding a live-model dependency to the demo. Independent attempts are useful candidates for adjudication; agreement between models is not proof. This corpus would strengthen bounded claims about reviewed fixtures, not establish live-model generalization.
Downsides
Human annotation and adjudication become the scarce resources. Freeze the reference before inspecting candidate outputs where practical; otherwise independence can erode. Novelty filtering matters more than example count. Any adopted gold-format or shared-contract change requires a dedicated task and decision under the repository rules.
Discussion test
Would a wrong but internally consistent evidence locator fail against an independently authored reference?

4. A first contribution that changes one outcome

Contributor path from curiosity to merged contributionConfidence 90%Complexity Low
Description
Offer a small, maintained set of starter challenges in existing packs: one contradictory artifact, one independently justified evidence span, one missing expected outcome, or one source-backed documentation correction. Each challenge explains the behavior under discussion, the bounded file set and the evidence a reviewer needs. A practitioner can first submit a synthetic counterexample or competing interpretation as an issue; maintainers settle the intended behavior before asking for a full fixture PR. Credit the accepted contribution in its associated case story.
Basis
direct: docs/CONTRIBUTING.md:11 leads with broad foundational reading, while docs/CONTRIBUTING.md:51 already welcomes fixture, gold, test and documentation improvements. A scaffold CLI already exists and is not the new idea here. external: Sigma’s contribution checklist provides a bounded knowledge-artifact contribution pattern. reasoned: A concrete outcome and review envelope give a newcomer a comprehensible finish line.
Rationale
This directly serves the goal of genuine GitHub contributions. People can contribute domain reasoning without designing an entire pack or learning every package first. Scope-specific guidance complements the authoritative rules; it does not silently waive them. The public casebook gives a contribution a visible consequence.
Downsides
Someone must curate and review the challenges. Low setup burden does not mean low review burden; prevent a flood of repetitive generated fixtures. Do not promise a completion time before testing the path with a newcomer. Publish issues or credit only through a separately authorized action.
Discussion test
Can a newcomer describe what their contribution changes and can a maintainer judge it from one artifact and one expected outcome?

5. A proof ledger with denominators and limits

Technical credibility and learning from failuresConfidence 90%Complexity Medium
Description
Replace an isolated perfect-score headline with a concise ledger of public claims: the guarantee, its exact verifying check, applicable sample count, revision and what the check cannot establish. Distinguish deterministic extraction agreement, real Postgres approval enforcement, independently annotated evidence and any future live-provider evaluation. Include a controlled failure example for selected claims, using the sabotage tests already present. Unmeasured and inapplicable dimensions should be visible rather than looking like additional successes.
Basis
direct: The landing trust chips advertise 1.000 (apps/web/src/app/page.tsx:144). The scorer returns 1 when total is zero (packages/evals/src/scoring.ts:338); this inspection does not establish that a current dimension actually has zero samples. docs/EVALUATION.md:157 explicitly limits deterministic-provider thresholds, and scorer sabotage tests already exist. reasoned: Claim-specific scope and denominators let readers assess what a score proves.
Rationale
The repository’s real governance tests deserve clearer presentation than a single number. This also exposes useful contribution opportunities and reduces drift between public claims and shipped checks. It supports the challenge corpus by explaining exactly which new evidence would strengthen a claim.
Downsides
A ledger needs stable links and revision-specific facts. Generating test output is easier than deciding what it establishes; editorial review remains necessary. Avoid turning it into a new analytics platform or a general safety certification.
Discussion test
Can a skeptic tell which promise was tested, on how many cases, against whose reference, and what remains unknown?

6. Take away the decision, evidence and next action

Visitor experiment and memorable operational storyConfidence 85%Complexity Medium
Description
Extend the shipped copyable summary into an operational handoff: what was approved, rejected or left pending; the supporting passage; the rule/version; human authorization; unresolved uncertainty; and the next action. Use the actual workspace outcome, not the tour’s step index, to describe what happened. Keep it stateless and synthetic, with enough context for a colleague to understand the reasoning without reopening the guest session.
Basis
direct: apps/web/src/components/tour/DemoSummary.tsx:11 currently models pack, scenario, entry and progress; its copied text contains those fields. docs/plans/2026-09-13-0705-feat-value-traction-upgrade-plan.md:43 already calls for a decision outcome in the takeaway. reasoned: An operational record carries the product’s value into another conversation; a progress counter describes attendance.
Rationale
This is a grounded completion of an existing idea, rather than a new share-link system. The casebook is a pre-demo reference; this receipt reflects what a visitor actually did. A reader gets a concrete reason to forward the result and return to the repository.
Downsides
There may be multiple decisions, a rejection, an incomplete journey or a reset. The account must represent those honestly. Avoid private workspace identifiers and links that imply continued access. Persistent share tokens and public live-workspace access are separate, substantially larger choices.
Discussion test
Could a colleague understand the outcome, its authority and the remaining uncertainty from the copied record alone?
